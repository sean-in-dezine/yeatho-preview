# Raw Milk, Goat Milk, and Dairy Self-Sufficiency in Texas

yeatho is farmer-coded and raw-milk pilled. That is not a slogan. It is a preference for milk that has not been heated past the point where the enzymes, fats, and bacteria that make it food are still present. In Texas that preference is legal under specific rules.

Texas permits the sale of raw milk for human consumption from farms that hold a Grade A Raw for Retail Permit issued by the Texas Department of State Health Services. Sales happen at the farm or by delivery in the producer’s own vehicle. Retail store sales are not allowed. Farmers markets cannot sell it on the spot, though a market can serve as a pre-arranged delivery point. The producer must meet herd health, testing, sanitation, and labeling requirements. Milk is tested at least quarterly for pathogens and antibiotics. Positive results result in the product being held.

Goat milk follows the same permit path. Goat milk has a higher allowed somatic cell count under the standards than cow milk. Many small Texas operations run goats specifically because the animals fit smaller acreage, produce milk with a different fat and protein profile, and can be managed with lower capital than a cow dairy.

This is not an argument that every person should drink raw milk. It is a statement that the product exists, is regulated, and is preferred by people who want milk from animals they can visit and producers who are accountable under a permit rather than a brand.

## Why the preference

Pasteurization kills the organisms that can cause illness. It also changes the milk. People who prefer raw milk cite the difference in taste, the presence of native enzymes, and the ability to buy from a known herd. People who do not prefer it cite the risk. Both positions exist. The site states the preference clearly: raw milk and goat milk from permitted Texas producers are part of the self-sufficiency picture yeatho supports.

## Texas practical notes

- Permit required for the seller. Buyers do not need a permit for personal use.
- On-farm pickup and producer delivery are the legal channels.
- Quarterly testing for listed pathogens and antibiotics is required of licensed dairies.
- Federal rules still prohibit shipping raw milk across state lines for human consumption.
- Goat and cow milk are both covered under the Grade A Raw for Retail framework when the producer holds the permit.

## FAQ: Raw milk and goat milk in Texas

**Is raw milk legal in Texas?**  
Yes, from farms that hold a Grade A Raw for Retail Permit from the Texas Department of State Health Services. Sales are limited to the farm or delivery by the producer.

**Can I buy raw milk at a grocery store in Texas?**  
No. Retail sales are not permitted. You buy from a permitted producer at the farm or by arranged delivery.

**Is goat milk legal under the same rules?**  
Yes. Goat milk is covered by the same Grade A Raw for Retail permit and testing requirements. The somatic cell standard is higher for goat milk than for cow milk.

**Do I need a permit to buy raw milk?**  
No. The producer needs the permit. The buyer does not for personal consumption.

**What testing is required?**  
Licensed dairies are sampled at least quarterly for specified pathogens (including Salmonella, Listeria, E. coli O157:H7, Campylobacter, and others) and for antibiotics. Positive results mean the milk cannot be sold until cleared.

**Can a farmer deliver raw milk to me?**  
Yes. Texas expressly allows a permitted producer to deliver in their own vehicle. This is one of the broader state rules on the point.

**Is raw milk allowed at farmers markets?**  
Not for on-the-spot sale. A market can be used as a pre-arranged delivery location for milk already ordered from the producer.

**What about herd shares?**  
Herd-share arrangements exist in Texas and are used by some producers. The core legal path for direct sale remains the Grade A Raw for Retail permit.

**Does this site sell raw milk?**  
No. This is writing about the preference and the legal framework in Texas. Actual purchases happen from permitted producers.

**Why does yeatho state a preference for raw milk?**  
Because the site is farmer-coded and raw-milk pilled. The preference is for milk that has not been pasteurized, obtained from permitted Texas producers whose herds can be visited.
