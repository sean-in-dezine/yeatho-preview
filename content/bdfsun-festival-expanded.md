# Beaver Dam Farm Sunflower Festival and the bdfsun App

Beaver Dam Farm in Buchanan, Virginia, has been family-owned since 1900. It operated as a tomato cannery in 1916, became a dairy in 1927, and exited the dairy business in 2019. Today it runs beef cattle plus hay and straw operations. The Sunflower Festival is how the family diversified income while keeping the farm working.

In 2015 Candace Monaghan (the festival founder) and her family planted 20 acres of black oil sunflowers simply because they looked good. The next year they opened the fields to the public. They expected a few hundred visitors. More than 1,600 showed up. The event grew from there. It is now billed as the largest sunflower festival and vendor show on the East Coast, drawing roughly 20,000 visitors annually across two weekends in September. An independent economic study put the festival and related activity at about $1.4 million in local impact in a recent year.

The fields hold roughly 700,000 black oil sunflowers. Weekend activities include hay wagon rides, a farm-animal petting area, 115+ handmade craft and food vendors, photo opportunities, and live music. Weekdays include field trips for preschoolers, seniors, and special-abilities groups, plus ticketed dinners. After the festival the sunflowers are harvested and the seeds are bagged as black oil sunflower birdseed sold at local stores. Seeds are also available for home gardeners.

The festival runs in September (2026 dates were September 11–20, with specific hours and special events). It is held on a working farm: gravel, dirt, and grass paths, limited shade, bugs present. Tickets are required; children five and under are free. Only leashed service animals are allowed. Alcohol is prohibited. Parking is free and managed on-site. Heavy rain can force closure; light showers do not. No refunds on weather closures.

## The bdfsun app connection

In 2021 the family needed a better way to handle festival logistics, vendor coordination, and visitor information. That is where the bdfsun.com project came in. The app was built to support the festival operations—ticketing flow, vendor management, event scheduling, and on-site information—so the farm could scale the event without the same level of manual overhead every year. It was completed in 2021 as a practical tool for a working farm that also runs a large public event.

The same pattern appears in other work: practical software for real operations (the Inorganic Ventures customer app that let people scan chemicals for SDS and CoA documents is another example from the same period). The goal was not a general consumer product. It was a specific system that made an existing farm business run smoother.

## FAQ: Beaver Dam Farm Sunflower Festival

**When is the Beaver Dam Farm Sunflower Festival?**  
It runs in September each year, typically opening the Friday after Labor Day and spanning two weekends plus selected weekdays. Check beaverdamsunflowers.com or the official event pages for the current year’s exact dates and hours.

**Where is it located?**  
13561 Lee Highway, Buchanan, VA 24066 (Botetourt County). Accessible from I-81 exits 162 and 156. Signs are posted at the entrance. It is held on a working farm.

**How big is the sunflower field?**  
Approximately 700,000 black oil sunflowers. The farm plants roughly 20 acres for the festival.

**What activities are there?**  
Hay wagon rides, farm animal petting area, 115+ handmade craft and food vendors, photo opportunities, live music. Weekdays include field trips for preschoolers, seniors, and special-abilities groups, plus ticketed dinners and other events such as goat yoga in some years.

**Is the festival pet-friendly?**  
No. Only leashed service animals are allowed.

**What if it rains?**  
Heavy storms can force closure. Light showers do not. No refunds are issued for weather closures.

**Can I pick flowers?**  
No. Unauthorized picking results in removal without refund. Cut stems are available for purchase ($2 each) at exit. The sunflowers are a crop; after the festival they are harvested for birdseed.

**How did the festival start?**  
In 2015 the family planted sunflowers on an open field because they were pretty. The following year they opened the fields to the public, expecting a few hundred people. Over 1,600 attended. It has grown to ~20,000 annual visitors and is described as the largest sunflower festival and vendor show on the East Coast.

**What is the farm’s history?**  
Family-owned since 1900. Tomato cannery in 1916, dairy starting in 1927, exited dairy in 2019. Now runs beef cattle, hay, and straw. The festival is one way the farm diversifies income.

**Is there an app?**  
Yes. The bdfsun project (completed 2021) was built to support festival operations—vendor coordination, scheduling, visitor information, and related logistics—so the farm could manage a large public event more efficiently.

**Who founded the festival?**  
Candace Monaghan. The farm remains family-owned and operated.

**What happens to the sunflowers after the festival?**  
They are harvested. Seeds are bagged as black oil sunflower birdseed and sold at local retailers. Seed packets are also available for home gardening.

**What is the local economic impact?**  
An independent study estimated roughly $1.4 million in local impact from the festival and related activity in a recent year. The farm also operates Gracely Hill, a wedding and event venue overlooking the property.

For current dates, tickets, vendor applications, and field-trip information, see beaverdamsunflowers.com.
