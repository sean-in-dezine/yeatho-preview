# Why Cornload Exists: Valuing Homes on Acreage (Expanded)

Every realtor comps properties. Pull recent sales, adjust for size and condition, land on a number. It works until the house sits on ten acres instead of a quarter-acre lot.

What does a pecan tree add? You can price crown molding or a kitchen remodel. A tree that takes a decade to produce anything doesn’t fit the same spreadsheet. Zillow and Redfin don’t close that gap. Their models learn from tract-home sales, and the facts that matter on acreage—usable land, well yield, septic, slope, access, outbuildings—aren’t in the data those tools rely on.

## Where the idea came from

The idea didn’t start with a developer. It started with a realtor who kept running into the same wall: the standard comp process breaks on landed property. Chris Traina wanted a different way.

I came at it from the contractor side. I ran my own businesses in Northern Virginia and Connecticut and spent years working with agents on inspections, contingency reports, and the fix-up work that follows. I watched value get added to houses in rooms and finishes. Land is different math. A slope that renders half the acreage unusable, a well that tests weak, a pasture that actually produces—those don’t show up the same way in a tract-home model.

## What the model does

Cornload estimates the value of homes on acreage, farmhouses, and rural homesteads in six Northern California counties: Solano, Yolo, Alameda, Contra Costa, San Joaquin, and Stanislaus. The core model learns from completed sales of homes on 1 to 160 acres in those counties. It does not train on ordinary tract-home sales, vacant-land sales, or live asking prices.

The model uses house size, deeded acres, age, bathrooms, location, and any recorded well or septic indicator, plus listing details when they’re available. County data feeds topographical views so two five-acre properties aren’t treated as identical when one is flat and buildable and the other is steep or partially unusable. The published estimate still uses recorded deeded acres. Usable acres are not a verified model input. That’s why a separate usable-acreage tool exists for owners who want to supply what the records miss.

Estimates are computer-generated. They are not appraisals, broker price opinions, or guaranteed selling prices. Cornload does not value working farms or orchards as agricultural businesses.

## Starting with six counties

Those six counties already produce more than 15,000 property pages, with roughly 12,700 estimates or ranges. The plan is to get the methodology right in a region where one MLS has clear coverage, then expand county by county. Scaling to fifty states would make the site the size of Zillow. Doing the smaller version correctly first is the point.

## Owners and the “bestiment”

Because the site is early, the team is building ways for owners to improve their own numbers. If someone can add a shed, a well detail, or another feature that doesn’t appear in satellite imagery or county records, that information makes the estimate more useful before a realtor ever comps the property. The working name for that better number is a bestiment. A simple property-claim process is in development.

## What it won’t do

Cornload stays in one lane: homes on acreage. No tract-home valuations. No vacant-land pricing as the primary product. The accuracy figures published on the site compare the model against itself under different data conditions (county records only vs. with MLS listing details). They are not claims against other tools. Typical miss on held-out tests has been in the mid-teens percentage range depending on data available; ranges aim to contain about eight in ten comparable sales.

## FAQ: Cornload and country property valuation

**What is Cornload?**  
Cornload is a computer-generated estimate service for homes on acreage, farmhouses, and rural homesteads in six Northern California counties (Solano, Yolo, Alameda, Contra Costa, San Joaquin, Stanislaus). It compares recent sales of similar country properties and uses county records. Estimates are not appraisals.

**Which counties does Cornload cover?**  
Solano, Yolo, Alameda, Contra Costa, San Joaquin, and Stanislaus counties in Northern California. Coverage means property pages and estimates exist for many parcels in those counties; not every address is guaranteed.

**Is a Cornload estimate an appraisal?**  
No. It is computer-generated from sales data and recorded facts. It is a starting point, not an inspection, appraisal, or guaranteed price.

**Why do standard home-value sites miss on acreage?**  
They primarily train on tract-home sales. Extra acres, wells, septic, usable land, slope, and outbuildings rarely move the number the way they do in actual country sales. Cornload trains only on sales of homes on 1–160 acres.

**Does Cornload account for usable acres?**  
The published estimate uses recorded/deeded acres. Usable (flat, irrigable, buildable) land is not a verified model input. Two properties with the same deeded acreage can differ significantly if one has more usable land. Owners can supply additional details via tools in development.

**What data does the model use?**  
Completed sales of homes on 1–160 acres in the six counties, plus recorded facts (house size, deeded acres, age, bathrooms, location). Listing details (wells, septic indicators, features) improve the estimate when available. It does not use live asking prices or ordinary tract-home sales.

**How accurate is it?**  
Held-out tests show a typical miss of roughly 17% on county-records-only estimates and lower when MLS listing details are known. Ranges are designed so about 80% of comparable sales fall inside them. See the accuracy page on cornload.com for the current published figures.

**Can owners improve their estimate?**  
Yes, in principle. The team is building owner-contribution features (“bestiment”) so details like outbuildings, well specifics, or production features not in county records can be added. A claim process is in development.

**Does Cornload value working farms or orchards?**  
No. It focuses on homes on acreage and rural homesteads. It does not value agricultural businesses.

**Where can I try it?**  
Search an address or parcel number at cornload.com if the property is in one of the six covered counties.

If you own a home on acreage in Solano, Yolo, Alameda, Contra Costa, San Joaquin, or Stanislaus County, run the estimate.
